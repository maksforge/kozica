# Play store listing copy

**This document is the only copy of the listing text.** The Console is where it is pasted, not where
it lives — a value that exists only in a web form cannot be reviewed, translated as a set, or
recovered when someone edits it by accident.

Three scripts read this file, so its headings are load-bearing:

| Script | Reads |
| --- | --- |
| `scripts/play-whatsnew.py` | the `## Release notes` section, rendering every locale's note in the one format the Console's textarea accepts |
| `scripts/notes-gate.py` | the newest `### x.y.z` heading, refusing a merge where it does not match `versionName` |
| `scripts/play-metadata.py` | the whole thing, generating the `fastlane/metadata/` tree the publish workflow uploads |

> **The gate exists because of a real failure.** A release shipped to production carrying the
> *previous* version's release notes: `versionName` had been bumped and the notes had not, nothing
> compared the two, and Play does not allow release notes to be edited on a live release. The
> correction had to wait for the next upload. Renaming a heading is cheaper.

### Full description — 2378/4000

```
Kozica is a hiking map of the Polish mountains, from the Karkonosze to the Bieszczady and across the Czech and Slovak borders where the ranges do. Every range arrives with the app, and the map works with no signal at all.

Every marked trail is drawn in the colour on its signposts — red, blue, green, yellow or black — with its symbol along the line. Where two trails share a path, both are drawn side by side, so you can see which one you are on.

The map also shows paths, huts and shelters, peaks, passes, springs, viewpoints, car parks and stops, national-park boundaries and the state border, in a light and a dark style. Shading and contour lines every 20 metres show the shape of the mountains.

Tap a trail to see all of it, with its name, length, ascent and descent each way, and its profile. Search finds peaks, huts, passes, villages and trails by name.

Plan a route: pick points from search or press and hold on the map, and Kozica draws it over the marked trails only, with its length, ascent and descent, the walking time to each point, worked out from the rule on Polish signposts, an elevation profile and a warning at every demanding stretch. One button plans a route along a whole trail. Save routes under a name.

Record a hike with the screen off and the phone in your pocket, with Pause and Stop in the notification. Each hike keeps its track, distance, times, ascent, descent and profile, a note, and photos from the camera or picked afterwards, pinned where they were taken. Export hikes, routes and trails as GPX, and back everything up to one file, photos included, to restore on any phone.

Tap the position button to see where you are, with the accuracy and the direction you are facing. Your location stays on your phone: Kozica has no permission to use the internet, no account and no ads, and it collects nothing.

The Safety button opens 112 and, for Poland, Slovakia and Czechia, the mountain-rescue number, the rescue app, the avalanche bulletin and the mountain weather. Kozica types the number into your phone app and you place the call. The rescue app opens directly, or on Google Play if it is not installed, and the bulletin and the weather open in your browser. The apps and pages need signal.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License. Relief from GUGiK, ÚGKK SR (CC BY 4.0) and ČÚZK (CC BY 4.0).
```

## Release notes (Play's "What's new", 500 chars per locale)

**Don't paste these by hand.** The Console takes all languages in one textarea, tagged by locale,
and `scripts/play-whatsnew.py` renders exactly that from the blocks below:

```bash
python3 scripts/play-whatsnew.py                 # newest version here, to stdout
python3 scripts/play-whatsnew.py --version 0.1.0 # a named one
```

It reformats and never retypes, so this document stays the only copy. It exits non-zero if a note is
over 500 characters, or if a language the **app** ships has no note at all — both of which Play
accepts in silence and a user then reads.

The label above each block is **parsed**, not decorative: `**<Language>** — <n>/500:` on a
line of its own, with the fenced note under it. The count is what keeps a note from reaching Play
truncated, and `play-whatsnew.py` refuses a language the app ships and this section does not.

**Write these about what changed for the person reading them.** A schema migration is not a release
note; the feature it made possible is. A release where nothing user-visible changed still needs a
heading — rename it and say so in one line, so the decision is deliberate rather than made by
default.

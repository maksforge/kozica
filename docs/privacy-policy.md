# Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 20 October 2026_

Kozica is an Android app for hiking in the Polish mountains: an offline map of the trails, drawn in
their signpost colours. This policy describes what the app does with information about you. It is
short because the app does very little.

## The app collects nothing

Kozica has no account, no sign-in and no server. **The app has no permission to use the internet**,
so it cannot send anything anywhere.

What it keeps (your settings, including where you left the map) is stored **only in the app's
private storage on your own phone**, and is readable only by the app.

There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no third-party SDK that phones home.

## What the app can access, and why

- **Your location**, only once you tap the position button and allow it, to draw where you are on
  the map. It is read on the phone and used there, never sent anywhere, and the app keeps no record
  of it. The map does remember where you left it, in the app's settings: if you last used the
  position button to centre it, that spot is where you were. The settings are not part of Android's
  backup below, so it stays on the phone. You can allow only an approximate location, or none, and
  the map works without it.

## When you write to the developer

The **Help and feedback** screen in Settings has a row to report a problem and one to suggest a
feature, and Settings' Language section has **Something read wrong?**. Each opens **your own email
app** with a draft addressed to the developer. The draft carries the app's version, your Android
version, your phone's make and model, and the language you are reading the app in, so a report can be
acted on. You see every line before sending, can change or delete any of it, and nothing is sent
unless you send it yourself. The screen's third row, rating the app on Google Play, opens Play and
sends no mail.

## The two things that happen outside the app

These are done by the operating system or by Google, not by Kozica, but they are the honest answer to
"where could my data be":

1. **Android's automatic backup.** If it is enabled on your phone, Android backs up the app's data
   to your own Google Drive, in a space that does not count against your storage quota and that
   Google states apps cannot read. You can turn this off in your phone's settings.
2. **Google Play.** Installing and updating an app is a transaction between your phone and Google,
   with its own privacy policy. The developer of this app receives aggregate, anonymous statistics —
   install counts, crash rates, country-level breakdowns — and no information about individuals.

## Children

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone.

## Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored. There is nowhere else to delete it from, because
there is nowhere else it went.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the history of this file is public
in the repository.

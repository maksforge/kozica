# Phase 4 — The map shows the shape of the mountains: shading and contours under the Trails

Sequence lives in [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md); the live worklist lives in [`DOD.md`](DOD.md). This phase
works inside [ADR-0008](adr/0008-kozica-never-opens-a-network-connection.md) and writes
[ADR-0009](adr/0009-relief-is-built-by-us-from-the-three-national-lidar-models.md), which records
where the relief comes from. It leans on [`phase-2c.md`](phase-2c.md): every Region arrives with the
app, so the relief can be one pack. [`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md) owes nothing: "relief", "shading"
and "contours" are what everyone calls them, and none names something the app means differently.

**What closing this phase means.** Today the map is flat: it draws the Trails, forests and water,
but nothing on it says where the ground rises, or whether the next kilometre of a red Trail climbs
500 m. After this phase the map is shaded as if lit from the north-west, with a contour every 20 m
and a thicker, labelled one every 100 m, across all nine Regions on both sides of the border. Under
it is a height grid on the phone, which phase 5 reads for a route's ascent and walking time. The
difference a device can be asked about: the Tatry at zoom 13 shows Kasprowy Wierch's 1900 m contour
under the red Trail, with no seam where the shading crosses into Slovakia.

**`PLAN.md`'s paragraph is right in what it promises and silent on two things decided on
2026-10-22.** The DEM is built by us from the three countries' lidar terrain models, once, not on
every release; and it ships as one pack for all Regions together, not as part of each.

---

## 0. What this phase inherits

- **MapLibre Native 13.7.1** draws a `raster-dem` source with a `hillshade` layer, Terrarium or
  Mapbox encoding, and since 13.0 the `hillshade-method` options and `color-relief`. It reads
  PMTiles since 11.8. Contours are not computed on the device: maplibre-contour is a GL JS plugin,
  so contours arrive as vector tiles.
- **Every Region is on the phone** (phase 2c), so the map never draws one Region's relief beside
  another's hole, and one archive for all of them overlaps nothing.
- **Regions overlap by a few kilometres** (phase 2b § 4). Two shading layers over the same ground
  would stack into a darker band at every seam; one grid has no seams to stack.
- **The pipeline's habits.** `build-map-data.py` pins its input, fails rather than build something
  different, and writes byte-identical output from identical input (ADR-0007). The relief keeps all
  three, but it is built once rather than per release: the ground does not change between pins.
- **The palette.** Every map colour but the five Trail colours is a tone of the scheme's palettes,
  generated by `scripts/map/gen_style.py`; the shading and contour colours join them.

## What is in, and what is deliberately not

**In:** a height grid for the union of the nine Region polygons, built from Poland's GUGiK NMT,
Slovakia's ÚGKK DMR 5.0 and Czechia's ČÚZK DMR 4G, at about 12 m (zoom 12). Contours at 20 m with
every 100 m line thick and labelled. One fast-follow `relief` pack holding both archives. The
shading, contour and label layers in the style, light and dark, always on. The credit on the map
and the three entries on the licences screen. The build, its pin, its own GitHub release and the
`publish-play.yml` step that packs it.

**Not in, and each with a reason rather than a phase:**

- **Copernicus GLO-30**, the obvious global source. It is a surface model: over the forests that
  cover most of the Beskidy, Bieszczady and Sudety it measures the canopy, so contours would bulge
  at every forest edge and phase 5 would add a climb at each. ADR-0009.
- **Mapterhorn's ready-made tiles**, which merge the same national models. Decided by the owner:
  the build is ours, end to end, as the OSM data is. ADR-0009.
- **A finer grid than zoom 12.** Zoom 13 adds about 29 MB for the Tatry alone, for gullies no
  phone screen shows at the zooms people pan at; MapLibre draws zoom 12's grid beyond its zoom.
- **A switch to turn the relief off.** Decided with the owner: it is part of how the map looks.
  If the shading is too heavy somewhere, the style is fixed.
- **Elevation profiles and ascent.** Phase 5's, which reads the same grid.
- **A per-Region contour interval.** The same hillside would change its line count at a Region's
  edge, and steepness would read differently from one range to the next.

**The tempting one** is colouring the land by height, green valleys to brown ridges, with
`color-relief`: no new data, one more layer. Out for this phase, decided with the owner, because
the map's colours already carry meaning (forest, meadow, scree, water and the five Trail colours) and
a height tint fights each of them where a Trail has to be picked out at a junction. **It is kept as
an idea, not a refusal**: once the relief draws, a tinted variant is a cheap experiment in the
debug build, to be looked at on the phone before anyone decides.

**The gate is drawing, not data.** What could veto the design, a height grid on the phone shaded
live, is whether a 2019 mid-range phone pans smoothly with a `hillshade` layer and a
contour layer over nine Regions' worth of vector layers. That needs a grid of the right shape, not
the right source, so A uses a Tatry grid made by hand, for the reading only, and never shipped.

**B is the expensive half and goes second.** Three agencies' downloads and a border seam are the
phase's cost. Taken before A, a bad gate would leave a pipeline built for a design it vetoed.

**B cuts no release; C does.** The relief reaches users only when the map draws it.

## 1. The gate: does the test phone pan smoothly with the relief drawn?

### The question, in the form that can be measured

On the test phone (Redmi Note 8 Pro, API 30), with all nine Regions ready and a Tatry height grid
at zoom 0–12 with its 20 m contours, does a scripted pan across the Tatry at zoom 11 and 13 keep
its 95th-percentile frame time within 25 % of the same pan without the relief, with no more frames
over 100 ms than that pan has?

**The statistic.** Each run is the same ten swipes at each zoom, on the same build and camera; its
p95 is taken over every frame the frame log records across them. The relief run is compared with
the baseline run taken straight before it. A frame over 100 ms counts against the relief only
beyond the number the baseline run shows: tile loads at zoom 13 over nine Regions can stall a frame
with no relief drawn, and the gate measures what the relief adds, not those.

### The apparatus

- **A hand-made Tatry grid**, the Tatry polygon's tiles at zoom 0–12, Terrarium, lossless WebP, 512
  px, as `relief.pmtiles`, with its contours as `contours.pmtiles` traced from it. Made with
  whatever is quickest (the planning's scratch extract from Mapterhorn was one): the reading is
  about the shape, and nothing here ships.
- **All nine Region archives pushed by hand** to `files/regions/<id>.pmtiles`, which
  `RegionSource.kt`'s debug overlay reads as ready, so no Region is `Failed` and phase 2c's progress
  line stays off the map. A `connectedAndroidTest` run's closing uninstall wipes them, so they are
  pushed again after one.
- **The debug half of a relief seam**, beside `RegionSource.kt`'s: the debug build draws
  `files/relief/relief.pmtiles` and `files/relief/contours.pmtiles` when pushed by hand. The release
  half returns nothing until C.
- **A frame log**, debug-only: MapLibre's frame listener on the map view, logging each frame's
  duration under one tag, and a pan driven by `adb shell input swipe` so both runs pan the same.

### The verdicts, decided now rather than argued about later

1. **p95 within 25 % of the baseline's, and no frame over 100 ms beyond the baseline's** → B and C
   as planned.
2. **Slower than that, but p95 under twice the baseline's** → shading from zoom 10 only, and the
   grid stops at zoom 11; read again. If the second reading passes, that is the design; if not,
   verdict 3.
3. **p95 twice the baseline's or worse** → the live shading goes: shading baked into a grey image
   by `build-relief.yml`, once, beside the height grid and pinned with it, and the grid still
   shipped, undrawn, for phase 5. This file is rewritten before B starts.

### The verdict, taken 2026-10-22

**Verdict 1** (R1): with the Tatry's relief drawn and all nine Regions ready, the test phone's p95
frame time stays within 15 % of the baseline at zoom 11 and level with it at zoom 13, and no frame in
any run passed 100 ms. B and C go ahead as planned. Frame time with the relief over every Region is not read again: the
map draws only the tiles on screen, so the grid's extent beyond the Tatry adds tiles off screen,
not work per frame; C's R3 and R4 read seams and delivery, not frame time. Two things the verdicts did not anticipate:

- **The relief runs render fewer frames per swipe at zoom 13**, about 37 against 49–58, at the
  same 16.7 ms each. It is not a weaker fling: one swipe from the same camera ends on the same view
  with and without the relief. The baseline keeps rendering after the motion has stopped, and
  those frames, at 16.7 ms, are inside its p95 and pull it down, so the comparison is if anything
  stricter on the relief.
- **The shading is too heavy** in steep ground with the gate's throwaway colours and exaggeration
  0.5: the Tatry's north faces go nearly black under the Trails. That is C's style work, read again
  in C's own screenshots, not a gate result.

## 2. The relief build

- **`scripts/build-relief.py`** builds `relief.pmtiles` and `contours.pmtiles` for the union of
  the nine polygons in `scripts/regions/`, clipped to the real state borders of Poland, Slovakia and
  Czechia: their `boundary=administrative`, `admin_level=2` relations, read from the extracts
  `scripts/osm-data.pin` already pins for the map build. Outside the three countries there is no
  national model. Geofabrik's `.poly` outlines would not do: they reach past the border on purpose,
  so extracts catch what lies along it, and would keep strips of Ukraine in the Bieszczady and of
  Germany beyond the Karkonosze that the gap check would then fail on by design.
- **Each agency's data, fetched by its own service**, only where it touches the clipped union, and
  **resampled onto the target grid as it arrives and then deleted**, so the disk holds small parts,
  not the source. What each agency actually serves was read on 2026-10-25, before the script was
  written, and differs from what this section first assumed (ADR-0009's amendment):
  - **Poland, GUGiK NMT**: the sheets of its WFS sheet indexes, one layer per survey year in the
    PL-EVRF2007-NH service and the PL-KRON86-NH one before it. **Chosen by ground, not by name**:
    the layers name their sheets in different schemes (1:5000 godła, and local ones such as
    `6.110.29.05`), so one piece of ground sits under several names. The build ranks every
    candidate, lidar ("Skaning laserowy") first, 1 m before 0.5 m (the same ground at a quarter of
    the download), then the newer datum, then the newer survey, and takes each only where no
    better one already covers the ground. Photogrammetric grids, the 5 m ones and many newer 1 m
    ones GUGiK marks "Zdj. lotnicze", fill only what no lidar covers, and in the merge only cells
    no lidar reaches: averaged in, they would blur it. For the whole union that is 5,041 lidar
    sheets (4,940 of 1 m EVRF2007 from 2019–2026, 100 of 2012–14 KRON86, one of 0.5 m) and 79
    photogrammetric ones; R2 records it. About 215 GB.
  - **Czechia, ČÚZK DMR 4G**: one zip per SM5 sheet from its ATOM feed, the feed's own polygons
    picking the sheets. Each holds a LAZ 5 m point grid, read with `laspy` and laid back onto its
    grid. About 1,500 sheets, 0.3 GB.
  - **Slovakia, ÚGKK DMR 5.0**: published only as one 197 GB archive, so the build reads ÚGKK's
    INSPIRE WCS instead, in 5 km squares at 5 m. Its heights are ellipsoidal, so each is corrected
    to Slovakia's Baltic heights with ÚGKK's own grid, pinned by hash: H = h − N, read at Kriváň as
    2537.22 − 42.92 = 2494.30 m against the summit's 2494.7 m. About 0.8 GB.
- **One grid, in one projection, with no seam.** Every sheet is reprojected from its national
  system (Poland's EPSG:2180, Czechia's S-JTSK, the WCS's ETRS89 / TM34) onto Web Mercator at zoom
  12's resolution, about 12 m at 49° N, averaging the source cells under each. Heights are summed in
  whole centimetres, so the merge gives the same bytes whatever order the parts finish in. Where two
  countries' data overlap at a border the mean is taken. Poland's KRON86 sheets are Kronstadt
  heights, like Czechia's and Slovakia's Baltic Bpv, its EVRF2007 sheets are not; all of them differ
  by well under a metre, and the build does not correct for it (§ Known gaps).
- **The build fails on a gap.** A grid cell inside the clipped union with no value from any sheet
  stops the build, which reports each connected gap with its size, a cell in it and its Region. If
  one turns up, it is filled from a named source for that gap only, in an amendment to ADR-0009, not
  by a fallback added in advance. Cells within 3 grid cells, about 40 m, of a state border are
  tolerated and counted: where the other side has no national model, OSM's line and the agency's own
  data edge part by a cell or two.
  **Water is the one gap found so far.** The first full build stopped on the Rozkoš reservoir in
  Czechia, about 1 km² ČÚZK leaves out of DMR 4G, as terrain models leave water out. GUGiK's grids
  fill their lakes at the water level (Morskie Oko samples at 1395.00 m), so the build does the same
  for water it names in `WATER`: each gap there that data surrounds is filled at one level, the
  lowest tenth of its shore's heights, since water lies at the bottom of its banks. Any gap not named still fails the build.
- **`relief.pmtiles`**: zoom 0–12, 512 px tiles, Terrarium encoding, **lossless** WebP. A lossy
  encoder rewrites the low bits, which are the heights.
- **`contours.pmtiles`**: traced from the same grid, so the lines, the shading and phase 5's ascent
  agree. A light smoothing before tracing so a 12 m grid does not draw staircases. 20 m lines with
  an `ele` attribute and an `index` flag every 100 m; the 100 m lines from zoom 11, all of them from
  zoom 12, nothing below.
- **Deterministic from its sheet lists.** Sheets in a fixed order, fixed encoder settings, no
  timestamps, pinned GDAL and libwebp. The sheets themselves come from live indexes, which gain a
  survey year at a time, so the build keeps the lists it used as `index-{pl,cz,sk}-<hash>.json`
  and the release publishes them; put back in `map-data/relief/`, they stand in for the live
  indexes, and a rebuild from them reproduces the archives.
- **Built once, not per release, on the owner's PC.** Decided with the owner on 2026-10-25: a
  one-time job of about 215 GB suits a home connection better than a hosted runner, whose six-hour
  limit a single run of it would pass. The script is committed and deterministic, `--device` binds
  its downloads to one network interface (the PC's Ethernet, not the test phone's tether), and each
  finished sheet is kept as a part, so a stopped run resumes. Its two archives, its three sheet
  lists and `scripts/map/RELIEF-DATA-LICENCE.txt` go to a GitHub release of their own,
  `relief-<date>`, and `scripts/relief.pin` holds that tag and both sha256s. Rebuilding is a
  decision, not a schedule: the ground does not move, and a national agency publishing a new survey
  is the reason to.

## 3. The relief on the map

- **One `relief` pack**, fast-follow like the Regions, holding both archives, about 200 MB. It
  never changes between relief builds, so a release that moves only the OSM pin costs nothing for
  it. `publish-play.yml` downloads the pinned release's archives, checks both hashes against the pin
  and fails on a mismatch, before `bundleRelease -PmapPacks`.
- **`RegionFiles` learns the `relief` pack**; it is not a Region, so it does not join
  `REGION_NAMES`, and phase 2c's progress line counts it as one more item: "Downloading maps — 4 of
  10".
- **The style.** A `relief` `raster-dem` source and a `contours` vector source, each added once,
  not per Region. Under the land cover's outlines and above its fills: the `hillshade` layer,
  lit from the north-west, its shadow and highlight tones of the scheme's neutral palette, lighter
  in dark mode; then the contour lines, thin at 20 m and thicker at 100 m; then the 100 m labels
  along their lines, under every Trail and POI label. Their colours are generated by
  `scripts/map/gen_style.py` from the palette, never written as literals.
- **The credit.** The map's line becomes "© OpenStreetMap contributors · Relief: GUGiK, ÚGKK SR,
  ČÚZK", with the OSM part still linked. The licences screen gains one entry per agency: GUGiK as
  the source, as Polish law asks; ÚGKK SR and ČÚZK under CC BY 4.0, each with its licence link and
  "Resampled, merged and traced into contours by Kozica".

Copy: "Relief: %1$s" on the map. The agencies' names and the licence titles are not copy. The
licences screen's "Resampled, merged and traced into contours by Kozica".

## Known gaps, deliberately

- **The three height datums are not reconciled.** Poland's heights mix PL-EVRF2007-NH and
  PL-KRON86-NH sheet by sheet, against the Baltic heights Slovakia and Czechia use; they differ by
  well under a metre. Against 20 m contours that is invisible, and correcting it would be a geodesy
  step with its own source to pin.
- **Relief stops at Ukraine's and Germany's borders**, a little short of where the OSM data does.
- **Every install carries about 200 MB more.** Accepted with phase 2c's decision to ship everything.
